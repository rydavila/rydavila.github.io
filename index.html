Rina Y. Davila Severiano

4th-year Ph.D. Candidate · Industrial & Systems Engineering · Georgia Institute of Technology

I build optimization models for the electrified economy. My research asks how electric freight and electrified supply chains can work with the power grid: shifting energy demand across hours and days, soaking up renewable generation, and making heavy-duty electrification pay off.

I am advised by Prof. Constance Crozier, whose group studies future electrified systems and how to exploit their flexibility. I am an Anderson-Interface Ph.D. Fellow and hold a B.S. in Industrial and Systems Engineering from NC State (summa cum laude).

Research

Operations research for energy and transportation: mixed-integer programming, decomposition methods and stochastic optimization, applied with real data to questions that matter for decarbonization.

Demand flexibility

Price-responsive flexibility from electrified supply chains

A MILP that couples vehicle routing, electrified production and power system planning. By exploiting the slack already present in supply chains, it reached 40% higher renewables utilization than battery energy storage.

  • Cement supply-chain case study showing multi-day, GWh-scale load shifting
  • Benders decomposition in Pyomo to handle integrality at scale
Freight electrification

Tipping points for heavy-duty vehicle electrification

When do battery-electric trucks beat diesel? I jointly optimize fleet composition, routing, charging infrastructure, supply-chain decisions and on-site solar for long-haul fleets.

  • Capital, operating, charging and payback analysis
  • Case studies across three geographic networks
Buildings & policy

Behind-the-meter solar and storage in Michigan

With the Michigan Department of EGLE, I modeled cost-minimizing solar and storage for buildings using NREL data, finding up to $10M in 25-year savings for large commercial sites. I presented the results to the Michigan Solar for All program.

Education

Generative AI and student learning

A statistical study in R of a two-exam crossover design: Mann-Whitney U tests, bootstrap intervals, question-level effect sizes and within-student paired analysis, used to inform AI-use policy.
